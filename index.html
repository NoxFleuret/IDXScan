Algorithm & Trading Engine Docs

1. The 6 Strict Recommendation Tiers

STRONG BUY Perfect Alignment Breakout

The highest conviction setup. Issued only when momentum, volume, and trend all confirm a powerful upward move.

  • Trend: Price is in an established Uptrend (Above EMA 50 & 200)
  • MACD: Bullish Crossover
  • Volume: Surging (Ratio > 1.2x average)
  • Oscillators: RSI is healthy (40-65) and Stochastics are bullish
ACCUMULATE Structural Bounce

Early signs of reversal. Highly attractive risk-to-reward ratio before the wider market notices the trend.

  • Structure: Price is exactly at Fractal Support or a Fibonacci Golden Ratio (61.8% / 38.2%)
  • Oscillators: Oversold (RSI < 30) or Stochastic Bullish Cross
HOLD Trend Continuation

The stock is trending up, but it is too late (or too hot) to enter new money safely.

  • Trend: Moving averages confirm uptrend
  • Caution: RSI is overbought (>70) or MACD momentum is slowing down
TAKE PROFIT Over-Extended at Resistance

The math indicates a pullback is imminent. Lock in gains.

  • Structure: Hit Fractal Resistance or 0% Fib level
  • Oscillators: Severely overbought (RSI > 70 + Bearish Stochastic Cross)
STRONG SELL Bearish Distribution

The structure has broken completely. Heavy institutional selling.

  • Trend: Total Breakdown (Below EMA 50 & 200)
  • MACD & Volume: Bearish Cross on High Volume
WAIT AND SEE Capital Preservation Mode

The ultimate safety fallback. The market is choppy, sideways, or giving highly conflicting signals.

  • Action: No Trades. Entry, TP, and SL will output as `--`. Stay in cash until clear direction forms.

2. The Mathematics

Dynamic ATR Stop Losses

Stop losses are not static percentages. The engine calculates a 14-period Average True Range (ATR). If a stock is highly volatile, your stop loss is mathematically widened to prevent getting prematurely "stop-hunted".

Fractal Support/Resistance

Instead of just pulling the lowest price of the month, the engine scans a 60-day window to identify literal "Swing Highs" and "Swing Lows" (price action pivots) to plot structural boundaries.

How to Read the Indicators

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

Measures the speed and change of price movements on a scale of 0 to 100.

  • Above 70 (Overbought): The stock has rallied too fast and is due for a pullback. Avoid buying here.
  • Below 30 (Oversold): The stock has dropped too fast and is due for a bounce. Good area to Accumulate.
  • Between 40-65: Healthy momentum for trend continuation.

MACD (Moving Average Convergence)

A trend-following momentum indicator that shows the relationship between two moving averages.

  • Bullish Cross: The fast line crosses above the slow line. A strong buy signal.
  • Bearish Cross: The fast line crosses below the slow line. A strong sell signal.
  • Histogram: Shows the distance between the lines. Growing bars mean gaining momentum.

Stochastic Oscillator

A momentum indicator comparing a particular closing price to a range of its prices over a certain period. Much faster than RSI.

  • Above 80: Overbought territory. Be cautious of sudden reversals.
  • Below 20: Oversold territory. Look for bullish crosses here for high-probability bounces.

Moving Averages (EMA / SMA)

Smooths out price data to create a single flowing line, making it easier to identify the direction of the trend.

  • EMA 20: Short-term trend. Prices tend to hug this line in a strong rally.
  • EMA 50: Medium-term trend. An excellent dynamic support level.
  • EMA 200: The Macro Trend. If price is below this, the stock is in a bear market.

Volume Analysis

The number of shares traded in a day. It is the footprint of institutional investors.

  • High Volume Breakouts: If a stock goes up on huge volume, institutions are buying. Very bullish.
  • High Volume Dumps: If a stock drops on huge volume, institutions are escaping. Very bearish.
  • Low Volume: Retail chop. Never trust a breakout on low volume.

Fibonacci Retracement

Horizontal lines indicating where support and resistance are likely to occur based on the mathematical Golden Ratio.

  • 0.618 & 0.382 (The Golden Pocket): The most common areas for a stock to bounce after a pullback.
  • 0.500: The halfway mark. Also a strong psychological support level.

How to Read the Charts

Japanese Candlesticks

Unlike a simple line chart, a candlestick tells a complete story of the battle between buyers and sellers over a specific timeframe (e.g., 1 day).

Bullish (Green) Candle

The closing price is HIGHER than the opening price. Buyers won the day.

  • Top Wick: Highest price of the day
  • Top Body: Closing price
  • Bottom Body: Opening price
  • Bottom Wick: Lowest price of the day
Bearish (Red) Candle

The closing price is LOWER than the opening price. Sellers won the day.

  • Top Wick: Highest price
  • Top Body: Opening price
  • Bottom Body: Closing price
  • Bottom Wick: Lowest price
Pro Tip: Long Wicks! If you see a very long bottom wick, it means sellers pushed the price down, but buyers aggressively bought the dip and pushed it back up. This is a very bullish signal (called a Hammer).

Support & Resistance Zones

The market has a memory. Support and Resistance are horizontal zones where price has historically had trouble breaking through.

Support (The Floor)

A price level where a downtrend tends to pause due to a concentration of demand (buying interest). As the price drops toward support, it becomes cheaper and buyers step in, preventing it from falling further.

Resistance (The Ceiling)

A price level where an uptrend tends to pause due to a concentration of supply (selling interest). As the price rises toward resistance, sellers lock in profits, pushing the price back down.

The Role Reversal: When a stock finally breaks through a heavy Resistance ceiling, that old ceiling usually becomes the new Support floor!
